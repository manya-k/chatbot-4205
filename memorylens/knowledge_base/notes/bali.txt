TRIP: Bali, Indonesia
DATES: July 2023, 10 days
TOTAL BUDGET: ~$45/day AUD

=== UBUD (Days 1-4) ===

Day 1 - Arrival & Ubud
Flew Jetstar from Brisbane to Denpasar — 7 hour flight, cost $320 AUD return booked 3 months ahead.
Grabbed a Blue Bird taxi from the airport to Ubud — 350,000 rupiah (~$35 AUD), 1.5 hours.
Checked into a small family-run guesthouse called Pondok Nyoman in the rice field area.
Cost 280,000 rupiah/night (~$28 AUD). Breakfast included — banana pancakes and fresh fruit every morning.
The room had a private outdoor bathroom with a frangipani tree growing over it. Magical.
Afternoon: walked down Jalan Bisma to the rice field terraces.
Tegallalang rice terraces — iconic, green, stepped down into a valley. Stunning even though touristy.
Got there at 4pm when the light was golden. Worth the 50,000 rupiah entry fee.
Watched the sunset from a warung at the edge of the terraces. Bintang beer (30,000 rupiah) in hand.
Dinner: nasi campur (rice with assorted small dishes) at a local warung on Jalan Dewi Sita. 40,000 rupiah.
Simple plastic chairs, fluorescent lights, best meal I had on the trip.
Emotion: immediately at ease, Ubud felt like arriving somewhere I had been before.

Day 2 - Monkey Forest & Cooking Class
Morning: Sacred Monkey Forest Sanctuary. 80,000 rupiah entry.
Hundreds of grey macaques roaming free through ancient temple ruins.
A monkey stole my sunglasses within 5 minutes. Lost them forever. 
Lesson learned: do not wear things on your head near monkeys.
The forest itself is beautiful — ancient fig trees, stone temples covered in moss.
Afternoon: cooking class at Paon Bali cooking school. 350,000 rupiah (~$35 AUD).
Went to the morning market first at 9am to buy ingredients with the teacher.
Learned to make: satay lilit, lawar, black rice pudding, and sambal matah.
The sambal matah — raw shallots, lemongrass, chilli, lime — was a revelation. So fresh.
Ate everything we cooked for lunch. Sat in an open pavilion with rice field views.
Evening: yoga class at the Yoga Barn. 120,000 rupiah for a 90 minute vinyasa class.
Completely packed with tourists but the teacher was excellent.
Emotion: joyful, creative, deeply nourished.
Budget day 2: approximately $52 AUD — over budget but worth it.

Day 3 - Temple Tour
Hired a driver for the day — Made (pronounced Mah-day) from the guesthouse.
Full day driver cost 500,000 rupiah (~$50 AUD). Took us to 4 temples.
Tirta Empul: holy spring temple where Balinese Hindus come to purify themselves.
Watched the purification ritual in the sacred pools — deeply moving and completely genuine.
Entry 50,000 rupiah. Had to wear a sarong (provided free at the entrance).
Gunung Kawi: rock-cut shrines carved directly into a cliff face. 900 years old.
Had to descend 300 steep stone steps to reach it. Worth every step.
Entry 50,000 rupiah. Almost no tourists here compared to Tirta Empul.
Pura Besakih: the mother temple on the slopes of Mount Agung volcano.
Clouds were swirling around the volcano above us. Slightly eerie and spectacular.
Entry 60,000 rupiah.
Lunch at a local warung near Besakih: mie goreng (fried noodles) and es teh (iced tea). 35,000 rupiah.
Emotion: spiritually stirred, grateful for Made's knowledge and patience.

Day 4 - Rice Fields Walk & Night Market
Morning: walked the Campuhan Ridge Walk — a narrow ridge between two river valleys.
Free, 2km each way, through rice fields and jungle. Went at 6:30am to beat the heat.
Completely alone for the first hour. Just birdsong and the smell of frangipani.
This was the most peaceful moment of the entire Bali trip.
Late morning: Ubud traditional market. Full of crafts, textiles, offering supplies.
Bargaining essential — started at half the asking price as a rule.
Bought a batik table runner (150,000 rupiah after bargaining from 300,000).
Afternoon: Neka Art Museum. 75,000 rupiah. Excellent collection of traditional and modern Balinese art.
Evening: Ubud night market. Opens at 5pm. 
Sate, nasi goreng, pisang goreng (fried banana), fresh coconut. Total 55,000 rupiah for dinner.
Ate standing up at plastic tables. Surrounded by locals. Perfect.
Emotion: deeply content, Ubud exceeded every expectation.

=== NUSA PENIDA (Days 5-6) ===

Day 5 - Boat to Nusa Penida + Kelingking Beach
Took the fast boat from Sanur harbour to Nusa Penida — 200,000 rupiah return, 45 minutes.
Rented a scooter on arrival — 80,000 rupiah/day. Roads on Nusa Penida are rough and steep.
Kelingking Beach hike: the iconic T-Rex shaped cliff with turquoise water below.
The viewpoint at the top is jaw-dropping — one of the most dramatic landscapes I have ever seen.
The hike DOWN to the actual beach takes 45 minutes and is extremely steep and slippery.
Made it to the beach — white sand, clear turquoise water, almost no one there.
Swam in the water. Clearest water of my entire life. You can see the coral below.
Hike back up took 1 hour. My legs were shaking at the top.
Worth every second of suffering.
Lunch: simple warung at the clifftop. Instant noodles and Bintang beer. 45,000 rupiah.
Emotion: humbled by nature, physically exhausted, completely euphoric.
Budget day 5: approximately $40 AUD including boat.

Day 6 - Angel's Billabong & Broken Beach
Morning: Angel's Billabong — a natural infinity pool in the rocks at the ocean's edge.
At low tide the water is completely clear, you can see fish swimming below you.
Swam in it for an hour. Felt unreal.
Broken Beach: a circular cove where the cliff has collapsed forming a natural bridge over the ocean.
Manta rays visible in the water below sometimes — we saw two.
Free entry to both. Just need the scooter to get there.
Afternoon: Crystal Bay — calmer water, good snorkelling, beach bars.
Rented snorkel gear for 50,000 rupiah. Saw sea turtles within 10 minutes.
This was the most wildlife I have seen snorkelling outside of the Great Barrier Reef.
Caught the last fast boat back to Sanur at 5pm.
Emotion: overwhelmed by natural beauty, Nusa Penida was the highlight of Bali.

=== SEMINYAK & CANGGU (Days 7-9) ===

Day 7 - Seminyak
Moved from Ubud to Seminyak — hired a driver for 250,000 rupiah.
Seminyak is more upscale — beach clubs, boutique hotels, nice restaurants.
Checked into a guesthouse — 450,000 rupiah/night (~$45 AUD). More expensive than Ubud.
Afternoon: Ku De Ta beach club for sunset. Minimum spend of 200,000 rupiah per person.
Cocktails were 120,000 rupiah each. Expensive for Bali but the sunset was spectacular.
Music, beautiful people, golden light over the Indian Ocean. 
Felt slightly out of place but also enjoyed the luxury after 6 days of budget travel.
Dinner: Sardine restaurant. Fine dining in a bamboo structure over a rice field.
Had grilled barramundi with sambal and coconut rice. 280,000 rupiah for the main.
Expensive by Bali standards but genuinely excellent.
Emotion: pleasantly pampered, but preferred the authenticity of Ubud.

Day 8 - Canggu
Rented a scooter for the day — rode from Seminyak to Canggu (20 minutes).
Canggu is the digital nomad hub — cafes with fast wifi everywhere, yoga studios, smoothie bowls.
Breakfast at Revolver Espresso — best coffee in Bali. Cold brew 65,000 rupiah.
The cafe is hidden in a laneway, tiny, always packed. Worth finding.
Morning: Echo Beach in Canggu — bigger waves, more surfers. Watched the surfers from a beanbag.
Had a surfing lesson — 250,000 rupiah for 2 hours with an instructor.
Caught exactly 3 waves. Fell off all 3. Had the best time.
Lunch: Warung Dandelion. Massive smoothie bowls and salads. Very Instagram but genuinely delicious.
Acai bowl with dragon fruit, granola, coconut 95,000 rupiah.
Afternoon: Old Man's beach bar. The social hub of Canggu. Happy hour Bintang 30,000 rupiah.
Met a group of Australian travellers. Ended up staying for 4 hours.
Emotion: energised, social, loved the Canggu vibe but it felt like a Bali bubble.

Day 9 - Tanah Lot Temple & Last Beach Day
Morning: Tanah Lot temple — a sea temple perched on a rock offshore.
Spectacular at sunset but we went at 9am to avoid crowds. Much better.
Entry 60,000 rupiah. The temple itself inaccessible to tourists but the setting is incredible.
Waves crashing around the rock base. Priests performing morning ceremonies.
Afternoon: last beach afternoon at Seminyak beach.
Hired a sun lounger (50,000 rupiah) and read my book for 3 hours.
Body surfed in the waves. Bought fresh watermelon from a beach vendor (20,000 rupiah).
Sunset cocktail at La Plancha beach bar — colourful bean bags right on the sand.
Bintang beer 45,000 rupiah.
Dinner: final meal at a warung — nasi goreng with prawn and a fried egg on top. 55,000 rupiah.
Emotion: bittersweet, not ready to leave, already planning to return.

=== DAY 10 - DEPARTURE ===
Last morning: final breakfast at the guesthouse. Banana pancakes and papaya juice.
Airport: bought kopi luwak (civet coffee) as a gift — 180,000 rupiah for a small pack.
Flew home via Denpasar. Trip felt complete.
Emotion: deeply satisfied, Bali delivered everything I hoped for and more.

=== TRIP SUMMARY ===
Total days: 10
Average daily spend: $45 AUD
Favourite area: Ubud (most authentic) and Nusa Penida (most dramatic)
Least favourite: Seminyak (too commercial, too expensive)
Favourite meal: nasi campur at the local warung, Ubud night market
Favourite experience: Kelingking Beach hike and the Campuhan Ridge Walk at sunrise
Biggest surprise: how spiritual and genuinely Hindu Bali still feels despite the tourism
Would avoid: Kuta (avoided it entirely), north Goa level of tourist chaos
Wildlife highlight: sea turtles at Crystal Bay, dolphins visible from Agonda-equivalent in south
Crowd preference: strongly preferred Ubud and Nusa Penida over Seminyak and Kuta
Budget preference: happy at the $40-50/day range, mid-range accommodation
Dietary notes: mostly ate local warungs, avoided expensive tourist restaurants
Physical notes: Kelingking hike was the most physically demanding thing on the trip
Would go back: yes, immediately, next time would spend more time in Ubud and less in Seminyak
