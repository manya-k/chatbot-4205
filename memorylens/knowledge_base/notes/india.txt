TRIP: India (Rajasthan + Goa)
DATES: October 2022, 14 days
TOTAL BUDGET: ~$55/day AUD

=== DELHI (Days 1-2) ===

Day 1 - Arrival & Old Delhi
Landed at Indira Gandhi International at 6am, absolutely exhausted from the overnight flight from Brisbane. 
Took a prepaid taxi to the hotel in Paharganj for 450 rupees (~$8 AUD). 
Checked into Hotel Shelton — basic but clean, cost 1200 rupees/night (~$22 AUD).
Felt overwhelmed immediately by the noise, traffic, and heat (35 degrees in October).
First meal: butter chicken and garlic naan at a small dhaba near the hotel. Cost 280 rupees (~$5). 
Best butter chicken I have ever had. Rich, creamy, smoky flavour.
Afternoon: walked through Chandni Chowk market. Absolute sensory overload — rickshaws, spices, 
street food, fabric shops all crammed together. Loved it but nearly got hit by a motorbike twice.
Tried jalebi from a street stall — deep fried, syrupy, incredibly sweet. Cost 20 rupees.
Evening: Jama Masjid mosque at sunset. Stunning. One of the largest mosques in India.
Emotion: overwhelmed, excited, slightly anxious about the chaos.

Day 2 - Red Fort & Humayun's Tomb
Morning: Red Fort — impressive but very touristy. Entrance fee 600 rupees for foreigners.
Too many touts outside trying to sell guided tours. Felt a bit annoyed.
Afternoon: Humayun's Tomb — far less crowded than Red Fort, much more peaceful.
Beautiful Mughal architecture, lush gardens. Spent 2 hours just walking around.
Entrance fee 600 rupees. Worth every rupee.
Dinner: Karim's restaurant near Jama Masjid. Had mutton korma and roomali roti. 
Cost 650 rupees for two dishes. Legendary place, been open since 1913.
Emotion: more comfortable now, starting to love the city.
Budget day 2: approximately $48 AUD including accommodation.

=== JAIPUR (Days 3-5) ===

Day 3 - Train to Jaipur + Arrival
Took the Ajmer Shatabdi Express from New Delhi station. 
Train ticket cost 755 rupees (~$14 AUD) for AC chair car.
Journey took about 4.5 hours. Chai vendor came through every 30 minutes. Cost 10 rupees per cup.
Arrived Jaipur 2pm. Checked into Dera Rawatsar haveli guesthouse — beautiful traditional courtyard.
Cost 2200 rupees/night (~$40 AUD). Splurged a bit but worth it.
Evening: walked the pink city old town. Buildings literally painted terracotta pink.
Street food dinner: kachori (fried pastry with lentils), lassi, and samosas. Total cost 120 rupees.
Emotion: enchanted, Jaipur immediately felt more manageable than Delhi.

Day 4 - Amber Fort & City Palace
Morning: Amber Fort — absolutely spectacular. Hilltop fort with intricate mirror mosaics inside.
Got there at 8am to beat the crowds. Hired a local guide for 500 rupees — genuinely worth it.
Guide explained the history of the Rajput kings. Spent 3 hours exploring.
Entrance 550 rupees. Cable car optional for 100 rupees extra.
Afternoon: City Palace in the centre of Jaipur. Still partially home to the royal family.
Museum inside has royal costumes, weapons, art. Entrance 700 rupees.
Lunch: thali at a rooftop restaurant overlooking Hawa Mahal. 350 rupees for full veg thali.
Unlimited refills of dal, sabzi, rice, chapati. Incredible value.
Evening: Hawa Mahal at sunset — the Palace of Winds. Best viewed from the cafe across the road.
Chai and biscuits at the cafe while watching the light change on the facade. Perfect moment.
Emotion: awe, pure happiness, this is why I travel.
Budget day 4: approximately $62 AUD.

Day 5 - Jaipur Markets & Cooking Class
Morning: Johari Bazaar for jewellery and textiles. Bought a block-printed cotton scarf for 400 rupees.
Bargaining was expected — started at 800, settled at 400. Felt satisfied.
Afternoon: cooking class at a local home. Learned to make dal makhani, aloo gobi, and gulab jamun.
Cost 1500 rupees (~$27 AUD) for 3 hour class including all ingredients and the meal at the end.
Host family was incredibly warm. The grandmother showed me how to temper spices properly.
This was the highlight of Rajasthan for me.
Evening: rooftop bar at the hotel. Cold Kingfisher beer (180 rupees) watching the sun set over the city.
Emotion: deeply content, felt genuinely connected to the culture.
Budget day 5: approximately $58 AUD.

=== JODHPUR (Day 6-7) ===

Day 6 - Bus to Jodhpur + Mehrangarh Fort
RSRTC bus from Jaipur to Jodhpur — 5 hours, cost 380 rupees (~$7 AUD). Basic but fine.
Arrived in the Blue City — buildings painted indigo blue, looks surreal against the desert sky.
Checked into a small guesthouse with a rooftop view of Mehrangarh Fort. 1400 rupees/night.
Immediately went up to the fort. One of the most impressive forts I have ever seen.
Perched on a 125 metre high rock. The views over the blue city below are breathtaking.
Entrance 600 rupees. Audio guide included — very well done.
Spent 4 hours exploring. Could have stayed all day.
Dinner: mirchi bada (chilli fritters) and mawa kachori from a street stall. 80 rupees total.
Emotion: stunned by the fort, deeply impressed by Jodhpur.

Day 7 - Old City & Stepwell
Morning: Toorji Ka Jhalra stepwell — an ancient water tank recently restored.
Geometric beauty, symmetrical stone steps descending to the water. Few tourists here.
Free entry. Spent an hour just sitting and sketching in my journal.
Lunch: Janta Sweet Home restaurant. Had the famous Jodhpur mirchi bada and rabri (sweet milk dessert).
180 rupees total. Locals packed in here — always a good sign.
Afternoon: clock tower market. Bought saffron (300 rupees for a small tin) and dried rose petals.
The spice sellers let you smell everything — cardamom, star anise, turmeric, dried mango powder.
Felt like being inside a cooking show.
Emotion: peaceful, unhurried, Jodhpur was my favourite Rajasthan city.
Budget day 7: approximately $42 AUD — cheapest day of the trip.

=== GOA (Days 8-11) ===

Day 8 - Flight to Goa
Flew IndiGo from Jodhpur (via Mumbai) to Goa. Cost 4200 rupees (~$76 AUD) booked last minute.
Arrived Goa 7pm. Rented a scooter from the airport area — 400 rupees/day.
Stayed in a small beach hut in Agonda, south Goa. 1800 rupees/night.
South Goa is much quieter than north Goa — fewer parties, more fishing villages.
Had a grilled fish dinner right on the beach. Fresh catch of the day — kingfish.
Cost 650 rupees with rice and salad. Ate with my feet in the sand.
Emotion: instant decompression, this is paradise.

Day 9 - Agonda Beach Day
Woke up and swam at 7am before the heat kicked in. Almost no one on the beach.
Rented a kayak for 300 rupees/hour. Paddled along the coastline for 2 hours.
Lunch: a beach shack called Fatima's — prawn curry with coconut rice. 420 rupees.
Prawns were enormous and fresh. The coconut milk curry was fragrant and not too spicy.
Afternoon: napped in a hammock. Read my book. Did absolutely nothing. 
This is exactly what I needed after the intensity of Rajasthan.
Sunset: the beach turns orange and pink. Dolphins appeared just offshore. 
I watched them for 20 minutes. No one else even noticed.
Emotion: completely at peace, recharged, grateful.
Budget day 9: approximately $38 AUD — one of the cheapest and best days.

Day 10 - Scooter Ride to Palolem & Dudhsagar Falls
Morning: rode the scooter to Palolem beach — 25 minutes south of Agonda.
Palolem is more crowded but beautiful — crescent shaped bay with calm water.
Had a fresh coconut on the beach (60 rupees) and a banana pancake at a beach cafe (150 rupees).
Afternoon: tried to get to Dudhsagar Falls via a local jeep tour — 800 rupees.
The falls are stunning — 600 metres high, milky white cascading water through jungle.
Jeep broke down on the way back. Waited 90 minutes in the jungle. Oddly a great memory.
Dinner back in Agonda: a whole grilled lobster at a beach shack. 1200 rupees (~$22 AUD).
For lobster that was absurdly cheap. Ate it with garlic butter and watched the stars.
Emotion: adventurous, happy with small disasters, deeply satisfied.

Day 11 - North Goa Day Trip
Rode scooter 1.5 hours north to Anjuna and Calangute.
North Goa is chaos — loud, touristy, packed with Russians and backpackers.
Did not love it compared to south Goa. Too commercial.
Anjuna flea market was interesting though — hippy crafts, silver jewellery, vintage clothes.
Bought a hammock for 500 rupees. 
Had a fish thali at a local restaurant away from the beach strip. 220 rupees. Better than the tourist places.
Rode back south feeling glad I chose Agonda as my base.
Emotion: confirmed that south Goa suits me much better than north Goa.

=== MUMBAI (Days 12-14) ===

Day 12 - Train to Mumbai
Took the Mandovi Express from Goa to Mumbai — 9 hours overnight, sleeper class.
Ticket cost 620 rupees (~$11 AUD). Berth was narrow but slept fine.
Arrived CST (Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus) at 6am — the station itself is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
Gothic Victorian architecture, completely overwhelming at rush hour.
Checked into a guesthouse in Colaba. 2400 rupees/night — more expensive but great location.
Morning: walked to the Gateway of India. Imposing arch on the waterfront built in 1924.
Very touristy but worth seeing. Took the ferry to Elephanta Island (150 rupees return).
Ancient cave temples dedicated to Shiva carved into the rock. Genuinely impressive.
Emotion: energised by Mumbai's intensity, completely different vibe from Rajasthan.

Day 13 - Mumbai Food & Culture
Morning: Dharavi slum walking tour — controversial but educational when done ethically.
Tour cost 800 rupees, run by a local organisation that employs Dharavi residents as guides.
Dharavi is not just a slum — it's a massive informal economy. Leather goods, recycling, pottery.
Guide explained that many residents earn decent wages and have lived there for generations.
Changed my perspective completely on what I thought I knew.
Lunch: Britannia & Co restaurant in Ballard Estate. 
Had the legendary berry pulao — basmati rice with dried barberries, caramelised onions, and meat.
This restaurant has been open since 1923. The owner is in his 90s and still sits at the counter.
Cost 550 rupees. One of the most memorable meals of the entire trip.
Afternoon: Leopold Cafe in Colaba (famous from Shantaram novel). Had a cold Kingfisher and people watched.
Evening: Marine Drive at sunset. The Queen's Necklace — street lights curving along the bay.
Thousands of locals come here to sit on the seawall and watch the sunset. Joined them.
Emotion: moved by Mumbai, it has a completely unique energy unlike anywhere else.

Day 14 - Departure
Last morning: Samovar Cafe at the Jehangir Art Gallery for chai and toast. 120 rupees.
Browsed the art gallery — free entry, excellent contemporary Indian art.
Bought last minute gifts at Colaba Causeway market — pashmina scarves, spice sets, incense.
Flew home from Mumbai international. Already planning when to come back.
Overall emotion: India was the most intense, challenging, and rewarding trip of my life.
Would I go back: absolutely yes, immediately.

=== TRIP SUMMARY ===
Total days: 14
Average daily spend: $55 AUD (including flights within India)
Favourite city: Jodhpur
Favourite meal: berry pulao at Britannia & Co, Mumbai
Favourite experience: cooking class in Jaipur
Biggest surprise: how much I loved the chaos of Delhi by day 2
Would avoid: North Goa tourist beach strip
Dietary notes: ate meat throughout, no issues with street food
Crowd preference: strongly preferred quieter spots — south Goa over north, Humayun's over Red Fort
Budget preference: mid-range, willing to splurge on unique experiences
