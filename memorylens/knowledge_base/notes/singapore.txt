TRIP: Singapore
DATES: April 2023, 5 days
TOTAL BUDGET: ~$130/day AUD (Singapore is expensive)

=== DAY 1 - ARRIVAL & GARDENS BY THE BAY ===

Flew Scoot from Brisbane to Singapore Changi — 8 hours, cost $280 AUD return.
Changi Airport itself is worth arriving early for — it has a butterfly garden, a waterfall, a movie theatre.
Took the MRT from the airport to the city — $2.10 SGD (~$2.30 AUD). Ridiculously efficient.
Checked into a hostel in Little India — $45 SGD/night (~$50 AUD) for a private room. Clean and central.
Singapore is small — you can cross the entire country on the MRT in 45 minutes.
Afternoon: Gardens by the Bay. The Supertree Grove — 18 massive steel tree structures covered in plants.
The light show at night (OCBC Garden Rhapsody) is free and runs at 7:45pm and 8:45pm.
Stood in the middle of the grove while coloured lights pulsed across the trees overhead. Genuinely magical.
The two glass conservatories (Flower Dome and Cloud Forest) cost $28 SGD combined.
Cloud Forest is the highlight — a 35 metre indoor waterfall inside a glass dome. 
Lush tropical plants, mist, orchids. The most impressive indoor space I have ever been in.
Dinner: Maxwell Food Centre hawker centre nearby. 
Chicken rice from Tian Tian — the most famous chicken rice stall in Singapore. 
Queue was 25 minutes long. Cost $5 SGD (~$5.50 AUD). 
The chicken is poached, silky, served with fragrant rice cooked in chicken stock and chilli sauce.
Worth every minute of the queue.
Emotion: immediately impressed, Singapore is like nowhere else on earth.

=== DAY 2 - CHINATOWN, LITTLE INDIA, ARAB STREET ===

Morning: Chinatown Heritage Centre. Cost $18 SGD. Excellent museum about early Chinese immigrant life.
The reconstruction of the cramped shophouse living conditions in the 1950s was eye-opening.
Buddha Tooth Relic Temple in Chinatown — free entry, ornate gold interior, relic of Buddha on the 4th floor.
Left shoes at the door and walked through in respectful silence. 
Lunch: Chinatown Complex Food Centre. Massive hawker centre over multiple floors.
Had char kway teow (fried flat noodles with egg, cockles, bean sprouts) for $4 SGD.
Also tried rojak (fruit and vegetable salad with shrimp paste dressing) for $4 SGD.
Both exceptional. The cockles in the char kway teow were perfectly smoky from the wok.
Afternoon: Little India — Serangoon Road area. 
Mustafa Centre: a 24-hour department store that sells everything. Bought Indian spices to bring home.
Sri Veeramakaliamman Temple — technicolour gopuram (tower) covered in Hindu deities. Stunning.
Sri Veeramakaliamman is dedicated to the goddess Kali. Incense smoke, devotees praying.
Arab Street: Malay-Muslim quarter. Colourful fabric shops, perfume stalls, Middle Eastern restaurants.
Sultan Mosque — the largest mosque in Singapore. Beautiful golden dome.
Evening: dinner at Zam Zam restaurant near the mosque. 
Had murtabak — stuffed pancake with minced mutton and egg. $9 SGD.
One of the oldest Indian Muslim restaurants in Singapore, established 1908.
Emotion: amazed by how many distinct cultures coexist in such a small space.

=== DAY 3 - SENTOSA & UNIVERSAL STUDIOS ===

Morning: took the cable car to Sentosa Island — $35 SGD return for the cable car.
Sentosa is Singapore's resort island — Universal Studios, casinos, beaches.
Universal Studios Singapore — $82 SGD entry. 
Highlights: Jurassic World ride, Transformers ride, the Madagascar zone for the atmosphere.
Queues were manageable on a Tuesday. Spent 5 hours.
The park is smaller than the Hollywood or Orlando versions but genuinely fun.
Lunch inside Universal: laksa (coconut curry noodle soup) $14 SGD. Even theme park food is good here.
Afternoon: Sentosa's Siloso Beach — free entry. 
Not the most beautiful beach I have been to but perfectly pleasant.
The water is calm and warm. Lay in the sun for 2 hours.
Beach bars everywhere — had a tiger beer and watched the cable cars overhead.
Evening: returned to the city for dinner at a zi char (Chinese home cooking) restaurant in Chinatown.
Ordered: steamed fish with ginger and soy, morning glory with garlic, steamed egg custard.
Total for two people: $42 SGD. Excellent quality.
Emotion: Sentosa was fun but felt like it could be anywhere. The city neighbourhoods felt more real.
Budget day 3: approximately $185 AUD — most expensive day due to Universal Studios.

=== DAY 4 - COLONIAL DISTRICT & HAWKER DEEP DIVE ===

Morning: National Museum of Singapore — $15 SGD. Fascinating history of Singapore from Raffles to independence.
The story of the Japanese occupation in WW2 was particularly well told.
Fort Canning Park — free. Hilltop park with historical fortifications and gardens.
The spice garden recreates the original botanical garden planted by Raffles in 1822.
Boat Quay: riverside area with heritage shophouses. Colonial era buildings now housing restaurants and bars.
Clarke Quay: the main nightlife area adjacent. 
Lunch: Lau Pa Sat hawker centre in the CBD.
This is a stunning Victorian cast-iron structure built in 1894. 
Looks like a greenhouse, now houses a food centre. Had satay ($1 SGD per skewer) and laksa ($5 SGD).
The satay row outside at night is legendary but lunchtime was perfect.
Afternoon: the Esplanade — the Durian building (nicknamed for its spiky exterior).
World class performing arts centre on the Marina Bay waterfront.
Marina Bay Sands: the iconic three-tower hotel with the boat-shaped rooftop pool.
The SkyPark observation deck costs $32 SGD. Worth it for the view of the city skyline.
Infinity pool is for hotel guests only. Watched a guest swimming with Singapore skyline behind them.
Slight envy.
Evening: Newton Food Centre for dinner.
Chilli crab — Singapore's national dish. Mud crabs in a rich tomato-chilli sauce.
One medium crab costs around $55-70 SGD (~$60-77 AUD). Expensive but essential to try.
Ate with my hands, dunking fried mantou bread into the sauce. Incredible.
Also had orh luak (oyster omelette) for $8 SGD. Crispy edges, soft inside.
Emotion: stuffed, delighted, Singapore's food scene is world class at every price point.
Budget day 4: approximately $145 AUD.

=== DAY 5 - BOTANIC GARDENS & DEPARTURE ===

Morning: Singapore Botanic Gardens — free entry (National Orchid Garden inside costs $15 SGD).
UNESCO World Heritage Site. 160 year old garden in the middle of the city.
The Symphony Lake area is serene — locals doing tai chi, joggers, families.
The National Orchid Garden has over 1000 species and 2000 hybrids. 
One orchid is named after Princess Diana. Another after Michael Jackson.
Ginger Garden section was my favourite — an entire garden dedicated to the ginger family.
Brunch at Casa Verde restaurant inside the gardens — eggs benedict $18 SGD. 
More expensive than a hawker centre but the garden setting was worth it.
Last hawker centre lunch: Tiong Bahru Food Centre.
One of the oldest hawker centres in Singapore. Locals only, no tourists.
Had chwee kueh (steamed rice cakes with preserved radish) — $1.50 SGD for 6 pieces.
Had kopi (traditional Singaporean coffee with condensed milk) — $1.20 SGD.
Both incredible and incredibly cheap. This was my favourite meal of the trip.
Afternoon: Haji Lane — the hipster street with colourful murals and independent boutiques.
Bought a batik print notebook from a small stationery shop. $12 SGD.
Changi Airport: arrived 3 hours early to explore.
Jewel Changi: the indoor waterfall and shopping mall attached to the airport.
The Rain Vortex — the world's tallest indoor waterfall, 40 metres, in the centre of a glass dome.
Did not buy anything but spent an hour just watching the waterfall. Free and spectacular.
Emotion: genuinely sad to leave, Singapore rewards slow exploration more than fast tourism.

=== TRIP SUMMARY ===
Total days: 5
Average daily spend: $130 AUD (Singapore is the most expensive destination I have visited in Asia)
Favourite experience: Gardens by the Bay light show (free), Cloud Forest, chilli crab
Favourite hawker meal: chwee kueh at Tiong Bahru — $1.50 SGD and perfect
Favourite neighbourhood: Little India and Arab Street (most atmospheric)
Least favourite: Sentosa (too resort-y, could be anywhere)
Food highlights: chicken rice, char kway teow, chilli crab, murtabak, chwee kueh
Most surprising: how clean, efficient, and multicultural Singapore is in such a tiny space
Crowd preference: preferred local hawker centres over tourist restaurants, less crowded areas
Budget note: Singapore is unavoidably expensive but the hawker centres keep food costs very low
Would avoid: paying for overpriced tourist restaurants when hawker centres are superior
Transport note: MRT is perfect, never needed a taxi
Would go back: yes, as a stopover or extended trip — there is still so much to explore
Safety: felt completely safe at all times, even late at night
Dietary notes: ate everything including seafood, pork, and halal food — no dietary restrictions
