TRIP: Dubai, UAE
DATES: January 2024, 6 days
TOTAL BUDGET: ~$180/day AUD (Dubai is expensive but manageable if you plan)

=== DAY 1 - ARRIVAL & DOWNTOWN DUBAI ===

Flew Emirates from Brisbane via Melbourne — 14 hours total, cost $890 AUD return.
Emirates economy was excellent — good food, wide seat, great entertainment system.
Dubai International Airport is enormous. Immigration was fast — visa on arrival for Australians, free.
Took the Dubai Metro from the airport — $3.50 AED (~$1.50 AUD). Incredibly cheap.
The metro runs on a driverless elevated track above the highway. Futuristic.
Checked into a hotel in Bur Dubai (old Dubai) — $150 AUD/night. Budget end for Dubai.
The hotel was fine — clean, good location, nothing special.
Temperature in January: 24 degrees and sunny. Perfect. Summer would be brutal (45+ degrees).
Afternoon: walked to Dubai Creek — the historic waterway that bisects old Dubai.
Took an Abra (traditional wooden boat) across the creek — $1 AED (less than 50 cents AUD).
The abra rides are the best value thing in Dubai. Surreal to be on a wooden boat surrounded by skyscrapers.
Crossed to the gold souk — an entire covered market dedicated to gold jewellery.
The amount of gold on display is extraordinary. Shop after shop of 24-carat everything.
Bought nothing but spent an hour just looking. No pressure from vendors.
Spice souk next door — sacks of saffron, cardamom, dried roses, frankincense.
Bought saffron — 250g for 120 AED (~$50 AUD). Good quality.
Evening: Burj Khalifa. The tallest building in the world at 828 metres.
At the Top ticket (levels 124 and 125): $137 AED (~$57 AUD) booked online in advance.
The elevator takes 60 seconds to travel 124 floors. 
The view is incomprehensible — the entire Dubai skyline, the desert beyond, the ocean.
At sunset the light across the desert turns orange and pink. 
The Dubai Fountain show starts at 6pm — the world's largest choreographed fountain below the Burj.
Watched two shows from the observation deck. Jets of water 150 metres high, perfectly synchronised to music.
Emotion: stunned by the scale of everything, Dubai makes other cities feel small.

=== DAY 2 - OLD DUBAI & CULTURAL EXPLORATION ===

Morning: Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood (Bastakiya).
The oldest surviving part of Dubai — wind tower houses from the 1890s built by Persian merchants.
Narrow lanes, traditional architecture, art galleries, small museums.
Free to wander. Dubai Museum inside Al Fahidi Fort: $3 AED entry.
Surprisingly good museum about Dubai's history from a small fishing and pearl diving village to a megacity.
The contrast between the old Dubai in the museum and the skyline visible through every window is striking.
Coffee Museum: free entry, small, charming. History of Arabic coffee culture.
Had qahwa (traditional Arabic coffee with cardamom and dates) — deeply bitter, 
served in tiny cups, refilled repeatedly until you wave your cup to signal you are done.
Al Seef promenade: the restored heritage waterfront along the creek.
Mix of old-style buildings and modern cafes. Popular with locals on weekday mornings.
Lunch: Al Ustad Special Kabab restaurant in Bur Dubai. An institution since 1978.
Had seekh kabab, chicken tikka, and fresh tandoor bread. Total $35 AUD for two people.
The owner told us he has been making the same recipe for 45 years.
Afternoon: Jumeirah Mosque — one of the few mosques in Dubai open to non-Muslims.
Free guided tours at 10am (had to book ahead). Guide explained Islamic prayer, culture, architecture.
The mosque is beautiful — white stone, intricate geometric patterns.
Required modest dress — long sleeves and trousers provided at the entrance.
This was one of the most educational experiences of the trip.
Evening: La Mer beachfront area. Outdoor dining, beach access, families and couples everywhere.
Had grilled hammour fish (local grouper) at a seafood restaurant on the waterfront — 95 AED (~$40 AUD).
Fresh, simply grilled, with tahini and warm bread. Excellent.
Emotion: surprised by how much character old Dubai has, had expected only skyscrapers.
Budget day 2: approximately $140 AUD — one of the cheaper days.

=== DAY 3 - DESERT SAFARI ===

Booked a full day desert safari through the hotel — $250 AED (~$105 AUD) per person.
Pickup at 3pm, return at 10pm.
Driven in a Land Cruiser into the Dubai Desert Conservation Reserve.
The dune bashing — the driver aired down the tyres and drove at high speed over sand dunes.
Terrifying and exhilarating in equal measure. The dunes are enormous — 50-100 metres high.
Several people in our group felt nausick. I loved it.
Stopped at the top of a dune to watch the sunset. The desert at sunset is extraordinary.
The colour of the sand shifts from gold to orange to deep red. Complete silence except for wind.
Sandboarding — stood on a sandboard and slid down a dune face. Fell over immediately. Very funny.
Camel ride at the desert camp — 15 minute ride around the camp. $20 AED extra.
Camels are grumpy and lurch violently when they stand up. Held on for dear life.
The desert camp: Bedouin style tents, carpets, low seating, fire pit.
Henna painting (20 AED), falconry display, traditional music and dance (Tanoura whirling).
Dinner: enormous buffet of Arabic food — mezze, grilled meats, rice dishes, desserts.
Kunafa for dessert — shredded pastry over sweet cheese, soaked in sugar syrup. 
One of the most delicious desserts I have ever eaten.
The stars above the desert camp were extraordinary — no light pollution.
Lay on a carpet outside and looked at the Milky Way for an hour.
Emotion: the desert was the most memorable experience in Dubai. Nothing else came close.
Budget day 3: approximately $245 AUD — most expensive day but worth every cent.

=== DAY 4 - MODERN DUBAI & THE PALM ===

Morning: Dubai Marina — the most impressive modern urban development I have ever seen.
A man-made marina lined with 400-metre skyscrapers. Surreal.
The Marina Walk: 7km promenade around the water. Joggers, cyclists, brunching tourists.
Had breakfast at a cafe overlooking the marina — acai bowl and flat white, $55 AED (~$23 AUD).
This is the expensive modern Dubai. Everything costs roughly 2-3x what it would in Sydney.
Visited the Museum of the Future — the most beautiful building in Dubai.
A torus (donut-shaped) building covered in Arabic calligraphy that doubles as window openings.
Entry $149 AED (~$62 AUD). Inside is an immersive experience about potential futures.
Slightly gimmicky but visually extraordinary.
Afternoon: Palm Jumeirah monorail to the tip of the Palm — $25 AED return.
At the Atlantis hotel: Aquaventure Waterpark — $350 AED (~$146 AUD). 
Did not go in — too expensive. Walked around the outside of the Atlantis instead.
The building is absurdly kitsch — a pink resort with Atlantis mythology theming.
FIVE Palm hotel beach club: $200 AED minimum spend for beach access in winter.
Skipped it. Sat on the public beach at the base of the Palm instead. Free.
The views of the Dubai skyline from the Palm are exceptional.
Evening: dinner at Nobu at Atlantis. Splurge meal. 
Black cod miso and rock shrimp tempura. $85 AUD for two dishes without drinks.
The best Japanese food I have had outside of Japan.
Emotion: modern Dubai is impressive but hollow compared to old Dubai and the desert.
Budget day 4: approximately $220 AUD.

=== DAY 5 - GLOBAL VILLAGE & DUBAI MALL ===

Morning: Dubai Mall — the largest shopping mall in the world by total area.
Contains an aquarium (visible from the mall floor for free — full entry $130 AED).
The aquarium tunnel: walk through a glass tunnel while sharks and rays swim overhead.
Did the tunnel — $40 AED extra. Worth it. A 10-metre whale shark swam directly above my head.
Dubai Mall also contains an Olympic-sized ice rink inside the mall. 
Watched figure skaters practicing for 20 minutes. Bizarre and wonderful.
Had lunch at a Lebanese restaurant in the food court — mixed grill plate $65 AED.
Afternoon: Global Village — a massive outdoor theme park where each country has a pavilion.
Entry $20 AED. Open October to April only. Closes for summer heat.
India pavilion, Pakistan pavilion, China pavilion, African countries, European countries.
Each pavilion sells food and crafts from that country.
Had Iranian saffron ice cream ($15 AED) and Turkish gozleme ($20 AED).
The atmosphere is festive and family-oriented. Thousands of people from every nationality.
This felt more authentic and joyful than the luxury malls.
Evening: Dubai Marina waterfront dinner — shawarma from a street stall ($15 AED).
Then walked along the Marina promenade watching the yachts.
Spotted a superyacht with a helicopter landing pad.
Emotion: Global Village was unexpectedly the most fun afternoon of the Dubai trip.
Budget day 5: approximately $165 AUD.

=== DAY 6 - JUMEIRAH BEACH & DEPARTURE ===

Morning: Jumeirah Public Beach — free access, clean, calm water, direct view of Burj Al Arab.
The Burj Al Arab is the most photographed hotel in the world — the sail-shaped building on its own island.
It is 7-star if you can afford it. A meal inside costs $500+ AUD minimum.
Swam in the Persian Gulf. The water was warm even in January (23 degrees).
The beach is immaculate. Lifeguards everywhere. Families and tourists mixed.
Had my last shawarma from a stall near the beach — chicken shawarma in a fresh flatbread, $12 AED.
This was the best value meal in Dubai and I ate it almost every day.
Afternoon: a final wander through the spice souk to buy last-minute gifts.
Date boxes — enormous Medjool dates in beautiful gift boxes — $80-150 AED.
Bought two boxes for gifts at home. Dates in Dubai are extraordinary quality.
Airport: Dubai International T3 — the Emirates terminal.
The duty free is enormous. Bought a small bottle of Oud perfume — $120 AED.
Oud (agarwood) is the signature scent of the Gulf. Rich, woody, smoky.
Emotion: satisfied but not desperate to return. Dubai is worth seeing but once was enough for now.

=== TRIP SUMMARY ===
Total days: 6
Average daily spend: $180 AUD
Favourite experience: desert safari — the dune bashing, the sunset, the stars
Favourite area: Al Fahidi old Dubai and the spice souk (most authentic)
Least favourite: the luxury malls and beach clubs (expensive and soulless)
Favourite meal: seekh kabab at Al Ustad, kunafa at the desert camp, daily chicken shawarma
Most surprising: how much genuine history and culture exists in old Dubai
Best value: Abra boat ride ($0.50 AUD), shawarma ($5 AUD), Metro ($1.50 AUD)
Worst value: luxury beach clubs with $200 AED minimum spend
Would avoid: Kite Beach luxury clubs, overpriced tourist restaurants near Burj Khalifa
Budget tip: eat shawarma and falafel from street stalls, use the metro, skip the luxury beach clubs
Weather note: January is perfect (24 degrees). Never go in summer.
Crowd preference: preferred old Dubai and Global Village over the luxury modern areas
Would go back: maybe, as a stopover. Once was sufficient. The desert alone justified the trip.
Safety: extremely safe, zero issues at any time of day or night
Dietary notes: mostly ate Arabic and Middle Eastern food, excellent quality everywhere
Alcohol note: alcohol available in hotels and licensed restaurants, expensive ($30+ AUD per drink)
